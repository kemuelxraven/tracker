User Manual

A simple budget tracker for two separate wallets. You add money in, split it into categories, then log what you spend. There is no sign-up and no password — you open the page and start using it, and your records are saved on your own device as you go.

01Bank & Cash tabs

The two tabs at the top are completely separate books. Each one keeps its own budget, categories, expenses, money-in history, logs and reports — nothing crosses over.

  • Use Bank for money in your bank account or e-wallet.
  • Use Cash for the money physically in your pocket.
  • Whatever tab is highlighted is the one you are editing. The labels on the card follow it.

02Adding money

Your total budget is not typed in directly — it is the sum of every deposit you record.

  • Tap + Add Money on the dark card, enter a note (e.g. Salary), the amount and the date, then Add to Budget.
  • Every deposit is listed under Money In & Transfers in the same window, marked In.
  • Made a mistake? Tap ✎ on a history row to edit it — the total budget adjusts by the difference automatically.
  • Tap × to remove a deposit and subtract it from the budget.

03Budget categories

Categories are envelopes you divide your money into — Groceries, Rent, Transport, and so on. The amount you give a category is its allowance for one month.

  • Tap + Add Category, give it a name and a monthly amount.
  • Tap a category row to open it and see its expenses.
  • Edit renames it or changes its budget. Delete removes it together with its expenses.
  • + Add and − Deduct adjust that category's budget without touching your other categories.

04The monthly cycle

Your categories start fresh every month. The bar above your categories shows which month you are looking at — use ‹ and › to move between months, and This month to jump back to today.

  • A category budget of ₱5,000 means ₱5,000 every month — not ₱5,000 for all time. When a new month begins, the category is full again automatically.
  • Everything on a category card — what is spent, what is left, the progress bar and its list of expenses — belongs to the month shown in the bar.
  • Move to a past month to review it, or to a future month to plan ahead. Adding an expense while a past month is open saves it to that month.
  • Logging an expense dated in a different month is still allowed — it is simply filed there, and a message tells you where it went.

Changing one month only. Open a category's Edit and untick Use this amount every month to change just the month on screen — handy for a December with extra gifts, or a tight month. + Add and − Deduct always apply to the month you are viewing.

Your total budget at the top of the page is not monthly — it is the running balance of the account, all the money you have put in minus everything you have spent.

05Moving money between Bank and Cash

Withdrawing cash, or putting cash back in the bank, is not spending — the money is only changing pockets. Use ⇄ Transfer on the budget card so both tabs stay correct.

  • Pick the direction — Bank → Cash for a withdrawal, Cash → Bank for a deposit — then the amount, an optional note ("ATM withdrawal") and the date.
  • One tap writes both sides: the money leaves one tab and arrives in the other, so your total across the two never changes by accident.
  • A transfer is never counted as spending or as income. Your Total Spent does not move, and no category is touched.
  • Before you confirm, the window shows what both balances will be afterwards. If a transfer would leave an account in the negative it warns you, but still lets you record it.

Transfers appear in Money In & Transfers on both tabs, marked Out on the account the money left and In on the one that received it. Editing the amount or date of either side updates the other automatically, and deleting one undoes the whole transfer on both tabs — the two books can never drift apart.

In Reports, transfers are listed separately from Money Added This Month, so moving your own money around never looks like extra income.

06Logging expenses

Open a category, then fill in the note, amount and date and press + Log.

  • Spent — ordinary spending, gone for good.
  • Loan — money you paid towards a debt: an amortization, an instalment, a payment on something you borrowed. Choosing Loan adds a box where you pick which loan the payment is for, so the tracker knows how much of that debt is left. You can add a new loan right there with ＋ New loan.
  • Choose the type with the Spent / Loan switch before you press Log.
  • On any expense row, ✎ edits it and × deletes it.
  • Once a category is fully used, logging is locked until you raise its budget.

07Loans you are paying off

Both Spent and Loan entries are money that has already left your pocket. The difference is that a Loan payment is chipping away at a debt — so the tracker keeps count of how much of that debt is left. Tap Loans to see them.

First, add the loan. Tap ＋ Add a loan and fill in:

  • Loan name — "Motorcycle loan", "Appliance loan", "Utang kay Tita".
  • Lender (optional) — who you are paying.
  • Total amount to pay — everything you owe on it, so the tracker can show what is left.
  • Monthly payment (optional) — how much you pay each time.
  • Payment day of the month (required) — the day your payment falls due, for example 15 if it is due every 15th. This is what the reminder is based on.

Then log each payment. In the category you paid it from, switch the type to Loan, choose the loan from the list, and log it as usual. The payment counts against that category's budget for the month, exactly like any other expense — because you really did spend it — and at the same time it reduces what you still owe.

  • The Loans screen shows still to pay and paid so far across all your loans.
  • Each loan has a progress bar, how much is left of the total, and a list of the payments you have made with their dates.
  • The tracker watches that day for you each month. A loan reads Due on the 15th while it is still far off, Due in 2 days as it gets close, Due today on the day itself, and turns red with Overdue — was due the 15th once the day has passed with nothing paid this month.
  • The Loans button shows a number whenever a payment is due within a week or already overdue, so you see it without opening anything.
  • Log a payment and the loan turns green with Paid this month — the reminder stops until the next month begins, then starts watching again on its own.
  • When the payments add up to the total, the loan is marked Paid off. Filter by Active, Paid off or All.
  • Loan payments where you did not pick a loan are gathered under "Loan payments not linked yet" — edit any of them to attach it to a loan.

Deleting a loan keeps its payments in your records as ordinary spending; they simply stop being linked to a loan. Nothing you have already spent is ever removed behind your back.

08Savings goals

A goal is anything you are putting money aside for — an emergency fund, tuition, a new phone. Tap Savings to see them.

Add a goal. Tap ＋ Add a goal and give it a name and how much you need. A date is optional, but worth adding: with one, the tracker works out how much a month you need and tells you when you are falling behind.

Put money in. Tap Put money in on a goal and choose where the money comes from:

  • A category — it is recorded as an ordinary expense against that category's allowance, exactly like any other spending.
  • Excess money — whatever this account holds that your categories have not claimed. This choice only appears when there actually is excess. No category is touched; the money simply leaves the account, and shows up in Add Money history marked Saved, where you can take it back any time.

Either way the money stops counting as money you can still spend, so nothing is counted twice.

  • Each goal shows a progress bar, what is left, and one of On track, Behind, Date has passed or Reached.
  • The Savings button turns amber with a number when a goal has slipped behind or run past its date.
  • Already logged something that belongs to a goal? Edit that expense with ✎ and pick the goal under Counts toward a savings goal.
  • Deleting a goal keeps its deposits in your records — the money really did leave the account — and only unties them from the goal.

09Reading the summary card

  • Total Spent — everything you have ever logged, across all categories and all months.
  • Remaining — your total budget minus everything spent. This is the real money you have left.
  • Remaining (Categories) — what is still unspent inside your category budgets for the month you are viewing, added together.
  • Excess — you have money that is not assigned to any category yet. Short Budget — your categories promise more than you actually have. Balanced — every peso is allocated.
  • The eye icon beside the title hides all amounts, for when someone is looking over your shoulder.

10Arranging your categories

Above the category list is a row of orders: My order, Name, Remaining and Most spent. It appears once you have two or more categories.

  • Tap the order you are already on to flip it — A to Z becomes Z to A, largest becomes smallest. The small ↓ button on the right does the same.
  • Only My order is really yours. The other three are just a way of looking at the list, so switching to Name and back never loses the arrangement you made.
  • In My order, each card grows a ⠿ grip on the left. Drag it to move the card. On a keyboard, move onto the grip with Tab and use ↑ and ↓.
  • Your choice is remembered, and travels in a backup file.

11Searching your records

Tap Logs, or press Ctrl+K (⌘+K on a Mac) — or just / — from anywhere.

  • It searches everything, not only expenses: spending, loan payments, money added, transfers and money set aside.
  • Every word must match. Typing palengke sept finds only entries that have both. Matches are highlighted in the results.
  • Any span of dates. This month, Last 3 months, Last 6 months, This year, All time, or Custom… for your own From and To dates.
  • Both accounts at once. Tap Both accounts and an Account column appears.
  • Filter by All, Spent, Loan, Money in or Transfers.
  • Tap any result to open it. If it belongs to the other account the tracker switches over for you and keeps your search.
  • Underneath: money out, money in and the net for whatever you are looking at.

12Reports and Charts

Reports groups every month's spending by category and shows the money you added, what was spent, loan payments and the grand total, with all-time figures at the bottom.

Charts draws the same records four ways. Use the ‹ and › buttons at the top to choose the month; everything below follows it.

  • Where the money went — a ring of that month's spending by category, with the total in the middle. The six largest get their own colour and the rest are gathered into Other.
  • Month by month — total spent each month for the last year. A month with nothing logged is shown as a real zero, not skipped. The month you are looking at is marked in orange.
  • Money in against money out — what came in beside what went out, with a strip underneath showing whether each month finished ahead or behind. Transfers to your other account count as money out, because they really did leave this one.
  • Budget against actual — how far into each allowance you are. Every bar's track ends at the same allowance line, so a bar crossing it is over budget, and those turn red.

Hover or tap any part of a chart for the exact figures, and Show numbers swaps all four for plain tables. Hiding amounts with the ◉ eye also hides them here.

13Sending your records to someone

The backup file is for bringing the tracker back. These are for sending your records to a spouse, an accountant, or anyone else. They are at the top of Reports.

First choose the scope: Months — all of them or just one — and Accounts — this one or both. The line underneath tells you exactly what will go out.

  • Transactions (CSV) — every single entry: date, month, account, kind, category, type, note, amount and a signed amount.
  • Monthly summary (CSV) — one row per month: money in, transfers, money set aside, spent, loan payments, total out, budgeted and net.
  • Category budgets (CSV) — budget, spent, left and percent used per category, with an Over budget marker.
  • Print / Save as PDF — a clean printed sheet: month-by-month totals, budget against actual, savings goals, then every entry. In the print window choose your printer, or Save as PDF to get a file.

The CSV files open in Excel, Numbers and Google Sheets. Amounts are always written in full, even when the eye is hiding them on screen — a hidden export would be no use to whoever you send it to.

14Settings

Open Data ▾ → Settings to make the tracker fit the way you actually handle money.

  • Currency symbol — tap one of the ready symbols or type your own. Every amount, label and placeholder in the tracker changes to match.
  • Names of the two tabs — "Bank" and "Cash" are only defaults. Rename them to GCash, Savings, Wallet, Palengke money, anything. The names follow through the whole app, including the transfer window and the summary card.
  • Budget month starts on day — leave it at 1 for an ordinary calendar month. Set it to your payday and the budget month follows your salary instead: start it on 15 and a budget month runs from the 15th to the 14th of the next month, shown as "Aug 15 – Sep 14, 2026" in the month bar. Everything on the category cards, in Charts and in the date ranges follows that cycle.
  • App lock — a PIN for opening the tracker, described below.
  • Name of this tracker — the title and subtitle in the top-left corner, and the name shown on the browser tab. The corner mark shows the first letter of your title, unless you give it a logo.
  • Look of this tracker — colours, logo and background, described further down.

Locking the tracker. Tap Set a PIN and choose 4 numbers. From then on the tracker asks for that PIN whenever it opens, and locks itself again after the time you pick under Lock automatically after — 30 seconds, 1, 5, 10 or 30 minutes without touching it, or Never. It also locks if you leave it sitting in the background that long. To lock it yourself straight away, use Data ▾ → Lock tracker.

  • Change PIN and Turn off lock both ask for your current PIN first.
  • Your PIN itself is never saved — only a scrambled version of it, so it cannot be read back out of your records.
  • A forgotten PIN cannot be reset. Your records live only on this device, so nobody can unlock it for you. The only way back in is to clear this site's data in your browser — which erases everything — then restore from a backup file.
  • Treat the lock as privacy rather than a safe: it hides the screen from anyone who picks up your phone, but the records themselves are still stored in plain form on the device.

Settings are saved together with your records, so a backup file carries them to your next device.

15Backup, restore and recovery

Found under Data ▾. This is how you move your records to another phone or computer, and how you protect them.

  • Download Backup File saves one .json file containing both tabs, your settings, and your logo and background.
  • Restore replaces what is on this device with the backup's contents.
  • Merge adds the backup's records to what you already have, skipping duplicates — useful for combining two devices. A merge keeps the look already on this device.
  • Reset clears only the tab you are currently viewing; the other tab is left untouched.

If your records ever cannot be read. A save cut short by a flat battery, or something else writing over the tracker's storage, used to leave you looking at an empty tracker with no explanation. Now it says so, and:

  • Nothing is deleted, and nothing new is saved until you choose what to do — so the damaged copy cannot be written over by accident.
  • Restore the last good copy — the tracker quietly keeps a copy of the last version that opened cleanly, and offers it back to you with its date.
  • Restore from a backup file, or download the damaged copy to keep it safe, or start fresh if you would rather begin blank.
  • If only part of your records is damaged, the tracker opens with everything it could read and tells you exactly what was left out.

16The look of your tracker

The button in the top-right switches between Light and Dark, and your choice is remembered. The first time you open the tracker it follows whatever your phone or computer is set to.

Everything else lives in Data ▾ → Settings → Look of this tracker.

  • Colour theme — Default is the original look. Then Luxe, Ember, Fern, Harbour, Indigo and Plum, each with its own matching backdrop. Custom lets you pick any colour you like.
  • One colour sets the whole palette, and light and dark are worked out from it separately — so whatever you choose is right in both. A colour too pale to read is deepened automatically rather than left illegible.
  • Logo — choose any picture and it replaces the letter in the corner mark and on the lock screen. A square picture works best. Remove puts the letter back.
  • Background — Built-in uses the artwork that comes with your theme, My picture uses one of your own, and Plain turns it off altogether.
  • How strong the background is — your own picture starts faint on purpose, because the background is there to give the page a mood, not to compete with your figures. The slider takes it up or down.
  • Reset the name and look puts the title, subtitle, theme, logo and background back to how they started. Your records, categories, accounts and PIN are not touched.

Pictures are stored separately from your records, so even a large one can never stop your budget saving.

17Keyboard

Everything can be done with a mouse or a finger, but a keyboard is quicker.

  • Ctrl+K (or ⌘+K), or just /, opens search from anywhere.
  • Esc closes whatever window is open. With two open, it closes the top one first.
  • Clicking the dimmed area outside a window closes it too — but only if you both press and release out there, so selecting text inside a form and drifting off the edge will not throw your work away.
  • Tab stays inside an open window and cannot wander onto the page behind it. When the window closes, the keyboard goes back to whatever you were on.
  • Enter in an amount box logs that expense.
  • On a category's ⠿ grip, ↑ and ↓ move the category.

18Do I need internet?

Only the first time. Opening the tracker once with a connection is enough — after that it keeps its own copy of the page on your device and opens with no signal at all, on a plane, in a basement, anywhere.

  • A Offline badge appears in the top-right when there is no connection, to say the tracker is still working normally.
  • Not sure it is ready? Data ▾ → Settings → Works offline tells you in plain words whether the tracker has saved itself to this device, and how many files it has. If it says it is not ready, tap Save for offline now while you still have a connection.
  • Everything you type is saved to your own device instantly. It does not travel over the internet and it is not stored on any server.
  • Nobody else — not even the person who made this tracker — can see your budget.
  • You can add it to your home screen and open it like an app.
  • When a newer version is ready, a small bar offers to reload. Nothing is lost either way.
  • The backdrop for a theme you have never opened is fetched the first time you choose it, so pick your theme while you still have a connection.
Please read — your records can be erased

Think of this tracker as a notebook kept inside this one browser, on this one device. It is private, but it is also the only copy.

You will find the tracker empty if you:

  • clear your browsing data, history or "site data" — this is the most common way people lose their records
  • use a private or incognito window
  • open the tracker on a different phone, computer or browser
  • uninstall or reset your browser

If saving ever stops working, a red bar appears at the bottom of the screen telling you so — it means the browser is refusing to store anything (a private window, site data switched off, or a device with no space left). Nothing you type after that is kept, so tap Download a backup in that bar straight away, then fix the cause and tap Try again. The tracker never fails quietly.

If your saved records are ever damaged, an amber bar tells you instead of silently starting empty. Your old data is kept aside and nothing new is saved until you decide, so it cannot be written over. The tracker also keeps an automatic copy of the last version that opened cleanly and offers it back to you with its date — so even without a backup file there is usually a way home. You can also restore a backup, download the damaged copy, or start fresh.

The fix is simple: open Data → Backup & Restore and tap Download Backup File every so often — after payday is a good habit. Keep that file somewhere safe like your email or cloud drive. If your records ever disappear, or you move to a new phone, open the tracker there and use Restore to bring everything back exactly as it was.
